Mirror

Mirror learns your good posture, then looks for six seconds every five minutes (every 12 seconds during a demo). It notices your head dropping, leaning in towards the screen, hunched shoulders and tilting, and nudges you after two slouching checks in a row. Your camera light comes on each time.

The check runs on this laptop. No picture is saved or sent. Mirror keeps your baseline proportions, the last result, and daily estimates of slouching, desk time and breaks. This isn't medical advice.

Sit the way you want to sit: back against the chair, shoulders relaxed and back, head straight above your shoulders. Then press the button and hold still for six seconds.

Eyes and face

Every 20 minutes Mirror watches your face for 20 seconds. It counts blinks and notices drowsiness (eyes closing, yawning), whether you face the screen, restlessness and sitting too close. It speaks up when you look drowsy, or blink much less or much more than usual (much more often means tired or irritated eyes). It keeps the results only, never pictures, and never uploads them.


This is what the camera sees on your face, not how you feel: a frown can just be concentration.
